Guidelines

For every item you are told the alias (how the book refers to the character while the identity is hidden) and the hidden identity (who the book later says it is). Judge only the text shown. Do not use what you remember of the book unless a question asks about it.

Q0. Do you know this book's plot?

Answer before reading. Yes if you have read the book, seen an adaptation, or know who the alias turns out to be. This lets us check whether prior knowledge changes the other answers.

Q1. Does the highlighted reveal passage say the alias is the identity?

  • Explicit: a reader of this passage alone learns that the alias and the identity are the same person (for example "the Opera ghost ... his real name is Erik").
  • Implied only: it strongly suggests it, but never quite says it.
  • No: it does not identify them, or it identifies someone else.

Q2. Does the reveal read as a revelation or as a reminder?

  • Revelation: the passage is written as the moment the truth comes out (surprise, confession, unmasking).
  • Reminder: it mentions the identity as something the reader was already told earlier.
  • Can't tell from the passage shown.

Q3. From the earlier passages, could a first-time reader name the hidden identity?

Read the passages from before the reveal (the alias is highlighted). Imagine a careful reader who has read only these.

  • No: nothing points to the identity.
  • Suspect: there are hints, and a reader might guess, but it is not settled.
  • Confident: the passages make it clear who the alias is, even without the reveal.

Q4. Is this a valid item?

Yes if the alias is a real reference to a hidden person and the identity is a specific named character. No if, for example, the "identity" is a description rather than a person, the alias and identity are obviously different people, or the alias is used openly for the named character from the start.
