# EchoTales / CHIMERA: novelty summary for the professor (2026-10-16)

Full evidence, provenance tags and limits: `NOVELTY_REPORT.md` (v1.4+). Every number below is text-derived (the book's own reveal sentence located by exact search), synthetic, or label-free. **No number is human-labelled.** The exploratory test events and the confirmatory events were selected by a language model and are not owner-verified. This page is a summary, not a substitute for the report's limits section.

## The contribution (redefined 2026-10-16 night)
**Novelty, redefined (2026-10-16 night; supersedes the ranking in 8a and 8b).** The paper is a methods-and-measurement paper on **reader-faithfulness**: the output of a position-aware reading assistant at chapter c may depend only on chapters up to c.
1. **Definition and guarantees.** A two-part contract (input channel, parameter channel), the observation that they compose and that passing one audit does not certify the other, and elementary, property-tested guarantees for the repairs (versioning by evidence horizon has zero divergence for any prefix-only derivation; hiding beats doing nothing only when fewer in-window prefixes already hold the final value; prefix-decided gates remove prominence leaks).
2. **Derivation look-ahead is a design defect, found and fixed on two systems (strongest).** On the deployed narrative graph: 6.0% of 685 character-cuts diverge, the versioned repair gives 0, naive hiding is worse (82 vs 23). On the generic whole-volume LLM character card (pre-registered, 54 reveal events): the card names the hidden identity before the reveal in 18.5% of events for 7 to 8B models and 23.1% for 24 to 32B models **under cipher names no model has seen**, against 1.2% and 1.9% for the versioned card (strict text rule: 24.6% and 21.1% vs 0%); every one of five models is positive; a second judge agrees on 97.8% of labels (kappa 0.88).
3. **Input and memory leaks separated on one system with one oracle.** At the reveal cut the memory-only leak is +2.5 points (7 to 8B) and +0.0 points (24 to 32B) while the build leak is about 20 points: for these books the pipeline, not the model, is the main risk.
4. **Premature resolution under prefix truncation (parameter channel).** A text-derived entitlement oracle, a renaming probe with a memory-free validity gate, and the finding that plain renaming validity checks are memory-assisted on famous books; the effect is large on famous books (exploratory, +26.0 points for a reference model) and bounded below about 5 points on obscure books (pre-registered, three 24 to 32B models).
**Not claimed:** fame dependence of memorisation (Chang et al. 2023), name replacement and pseudonymisation (Jiang and Ferraro), point-in-time correctness (feature stores), look-ahead bias as a concept (finance LLM work). Prior-art search was bounded; "to our knowledge" applies.

## Channel 1 claim, stated narrowly
When a language model is shown a passage of a famous public-domain novel and asked who a disguised character really is, it names the true identity **before the book reveals it** far more often with the original names than with consistently replaced names. The effect is absent on unseen synthetic text, tracks how well-known the book is, and is **not explained by loss of name-borne cues**. It is **clear for the strongest model tested (Luna) and across the full 38-event set, and weaker and not reliably replicated for small local models on less-famous books** (a final pre-registered test on 7 fresh books came out NOT SUPPORTED for the small models). We call this name-keyed memory leakage; we do not claim it is the only mechanism.

## Found and fixed in a second system (pre-registered, 2026-10-16)
RF-E2E (pre-registered, addenda 6 and 7, written before any card was read): on 54 confirmatory reveal events, a whole-volume LLM character card stamped at first mention names the hidden identity before the reveal in 18.5% of events under cipher names no model has seen (judge rule; strict text rule 24.6% of 19), against 1.2% for the versioned (prefix-built) card. Input-channel RF1 SUPPORTED on the primary text rule (+24.6 [+8.8, +42.1]) and SUPPORTED on the judge rule (+17.3 [+9.3, +26.5]); memory-only leak +2.5 [+0.0, +5.6]. Pseudonymise-and-map-back: prefix-card leak 3.7% to 3.1% (RF5 not evaluated: no memory leak to fix). Models: llama3.1:8b, mistral:7b-instruct, qwen2.5:7b; not run: none. Larger open models (mistral24, qwen32b, addendum 8): W-ren 23.1% vs P-ren 1.9%; memory-only leak +0.0 points. This shows the look-ahead defect is a property of a common assistant design, not of our code, separates the input leak from the memory leak on one system, and shows the versioned repair removing it. Section 5.24 of the report.

**Positions and prompt (addendum 11, Qwen2.5-7B).** Rule A (text): the versioned card leaks in 0.0% at the half-way position and 0.0% at three quarters, while the whole-volume card, served from the first mention, leaks in 21.1% at every position; with the second prompt the input channel is +26.3 [+10.5, +47.4], robust to the prompt. Rule B (judge): the versioned card leaks in 1.9% at the half-way position and 1.9% at three quarters, while the whole-volume card, served from the first mention, leaks in 16.7% at every position; with the second prompt the input channel is +18.5 [+9.3, +29.6], robust to the prompt.

**Measurement checks (post hoc, no model calls).** Judge vs the text-derived rule over 380 card labels: sensitivity 91.7%, false-positive rate 0.9%, kappa 0.92. Exposure: a leaking whole-book card is visible for a median 11.4% of the book before the reveal.

**Robustness (pre-registered addendum 10, Qwen2.5-7B):** Rule A (text): with the instruction the whole-volume card still leaks in 15.8% (renamed) and 21.1% (original) against 21.1% and 26.3% without it; the remaining gap to the versioned card (+15.8 points) is not significant at 19 events (95% lower limit 0.0); with the second cipher the input channel is +31.6 [+10.5, +52.6], robust to the cipher. Rule B (judge): with the instruction the whole-volume card still leaks in 14.8% (renamed) and 18.5% (original) against 16.7% and 16.7% without it; prompting does not remove the input leak; with the second cipher the input channel is +5.6 [-5.6, +18.5], not shown robust to the cipher (the judge flags 7.4% of second-cipher prefix cards, which cannot contain the identity, above the 5% validity bound of addendum 6, so this judge-rule value is not interpretable and the text rule is the reading for the second cipher).

## Confirmatory run (pre-registered, 2026-10-16): the effect was NOT detected on obscure books
68 events from 65 public-domain books (primary set 32 events, 78% low-fame), three open 24B to 32B models (Qwen2.5-32B, OLMo-2-32B, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B), events mined and audited by models, no human labels. **Pooled gap original minus renamed +0.1 points [-5.0, +5.2] (H1 not passed); with pseudonyms +5.7 [-1.3, +12.6] (H2 not passed).** The closed-book control recovers the identity in only 2 of 68 events, so these books hold little memory to detect, which fits the dose-response above rather than contradicting it. The pre-registered fame contrast is +7.6 [-1.4, +18.3], p = 0.060, **not passed and underpowered** (minimum detectable 12.8 points); the fame correlation is +0.30 (raw p 0.041, Holm 0.145, not confirmed). Pseudonymisation lowers premature naming from 5.0% to 2.6% but costs 6.7 to 15.6 points of accuracy (a replication of Jiang and Ferraro's idea, not a new method). Recap spoilers (E5): none detected in 144 outputs per condition (a floor, not safety). OLMo training-data link: Spearman +0.13, p = 0.41 (null). Unseen stories (E2, 17): gaps about 0. The Claude arm stopped at 211 of 1,218 answers (subscription limit) and is excluded. Section 5.23 of the report.

## What is new (and what is not)
- **Not new:** renaming characters as a memorisation probe (prior work exists); chapter-prefix evaluation.
- **Plausibly new in combination (prior-art search was not exhaustive):** (1) a renaming-controlled test of premature identity naming in an alias-reveal setting, with a memory-free validity gate, a closed-book positive control and an unseen-text control; (2) the finding that plain-control validity checks on famous books are themselves memory-assisted; (3) a pre-registered cue-preserving test separating memory from name-borne cue loss; (4) a prefix-causality audit of a narrative graph pipeline (resolver exactly causal; persona stage looks ahead on 8.0% of character checks, 23 of 288), with the resolver shown to behave as a normalised-string lookup.

## What is not claimed
No evidence that the EchoTales pipeline itself leaks memorised identities. No human-labelled result. Not a general claim about all models or genres (public-domain Western fiction; five small local models plus one API model). Several earlier verdicts were INCONCLUSIVE or PARTIALLY SUPPORTED by the pre-registered rules and are reported as such; the strongest numbers come from a re-evaluation of already-seen data, and item-level intervals in earlier sections were too narrow (corrected to event-level in 5.19).

## Weak points to say out loud
Model-selected events; heuristic pseudonyms (gender from pronouns); a memory probe that gives the title; a small seven-event final test that did not replicate for the small models; the strongest evidence rests on one API model; six analysis versions (each pre-registered, none discarded); no independent human check.
