\section{PD v7: prospective familiarity contrast (local models only)}
PD v7 selected 9 events (5 from famous and 4 from obscure books) to test the familiarity contrast prospectively. Only 2 local models passed the validity gate (Mistral-7B, Phi-4-mini); their mean gap was -5.0 points on famous and +0.0 on obscure events (difference -5.0 [-11.4, +0.8], one-sided permutation $p=0.95$). The test was inconclusive: too few models were valid and the event counts were small. The reference model was not run on v7.

\mbox{}\\

\{WINDOW\}\\

\mbox{}\\

\mbox{}\\

\{CANDIDATES\}\\

\mbox{}\\

\end{quote}

\paragraph{Confirmatory event miner}
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\raggedright\sloppy
Below is a passage from a novel. Its last sentence may state that a person referred to by one expression earlier in the passage is in fact a specific, named person (a disguise, an alias, a hidden identity).\\
\mbox{}\\
PASSAGE:\\
<<<\\
\{PASSAGE\}\\
>>>\\
\mbox{}\\
If the last sentence states this, reply with a JSON object \{"alias\_surfaces": [the expressions the book used for the person before the reveal, copied exactly], "identity\_name": the person's real name as the passage gives it, "reveal\_sentence": the last sentence copied exactly\}. If it does not, reply \{"none": true\}.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Confirmatory event auditor}
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\raggedright\sloppy
Below is a passage from a novel, then a claim.\\
\mbox{}\\
PASSAGE:\\
<<<\\
\{PASSAGE\}\\
>>>\\
\mbox{}\\
CLAIM: the character referred to as \{ALIAS\} in the book is the same person as \{IDENTITY\}.\\
\mbox{}\\
Does the passage itself EXPLICITLY state this (the text directly equates the two, in narration or in a speaker's words), only IMPLY it, or NOT support it? Reply with a JSON object \{"answer": X\} where X is exactly one of EXPLICIT, IMPLIED, NOT\_SUPPORTED.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Character card (W and P builds)}

\end{quote}

\paragraph{Card judge (judge rule)}
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\raggedright\sloppy

Card: \{CARD\}\\
\mbox{}\\
Does the card state or clearly imply that the character called "\{ALIAS\}" is the same person as "\{IDENTITY\}"? Reply as JSON: \{"identifies": true\} or \{"identifies": false\}.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Long-context assistant with reader-position marker}
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\raggedright\sloppy

\mbox{}\\

\{CTX\}\\
\mbox{}\\
Reader's question: Who is \{ALIAS\}? Answer in at most 60 words.
\end{quote}

\mbox{}\\
\{PASSAGE\}\\
\mbox{}\\

\end{quote}

\paragraph{Rubric, examples and disagreement categories for the reveal check.} Annotators answered five questions, in this order, and saw model labels for none. Q0 asked whether they already knew the plot. Q1 asked whether the highlighted reveal passage says the alias is the identity: \emph{explicit} (a reader of that passage alone learns they are the same person, for example ``the Opera ghost \dots{} his real name is Erik''), \emph{implied only}, or \emph{no}. Q2 asked whether the passage reads as a \emph{revelation} (the moment the truth comes out) or a \emph{reminder} of something the reader was already told. Q3 asked, from the earlier passages only, whether a careful first-time reader could name the hidden identity: \emph{no}, \emph{suspect} (hints, not settled) or \emph{confident}. Q4 asked whether the item is valid, meaning a real reference to a hidden person whose identity is a specific named character and not a description. The disagreements fall into four kinds. (i)~Explicitness: both annotators judged the reveal explicit in 34 of 36 events, and in the other 2 at least one called it implied. (ii)~Inferability: at least one annotator suspected the identity from the earlier passages in 17 events and both did in 8, and none judged it confidently inferable. (iii)~Revelation against reminder: at least one annotator read the reveal as a reminder in 9 events. (iv)~Validity: at least one annotator marked an item invalid in 6 events, all confirmatory and each accepted by the other annotator (\texttt{C10150\_1}, \texttt{C1848\_1}, \texttt{C35443\_1}, \texttt{C39858\_1}, \texttt{C4537\_1}, \texttt{C7497\_1}); the event-level labels are in the repository next to the scoring script.
